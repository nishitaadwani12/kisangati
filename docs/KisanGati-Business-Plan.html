1. Executive summary

What KisanGati is: a technology layer on top of the family's existing tractor spare-parts shop in Jhansi, UP. Today the shop already has inventory, wholesale suppliers, phone-order clients, and bus-conductor delivery tie-ups. KisanGati lets villagers and mechanics order parts remotely (by WhatsApp voice note or photo — no travel), pay upfront by UPI, and collect the part the same day at their village bus stop, while the shop handles billing, matching and dispatch through simple software instead of by hand.

Why now: the hard part of rural business — trust, inventory, and last-mile delivery — already exists in the shop. KisanGati digitizes the ordering and paperwork, so the same shop can serve far more villages without more staff, and lays the groundwork for a proprietary AI that identifies a part from a farmer's photo.

The core promise we prove first: a farmer sends a WhatsApp message → the shop sees it instantly with the part matched → the farmer pays by UPI → the part goes on the next bus with a secret 4-digit code. Prove that loop with real orders under the existing shop name before spending on apps, lawyers, or advertising.

2. The problem we solve

  • Farmers lose time and crop money. When a tractor breaks during sowing/harvest, the farmer often travels hours to town, may not know the exact part, and loses a working day.
  • Farmers can't name the part. Many don't know brand, model, or specs (how many teeth a gear has, etc.) — they only have the broken piece in hand.
  • The shop is limited by manual work. Phone orders, handwritten bills, and manual routing cap how many villages one shop can serve.
  • Cash-on-delivery is risky — non-pickup and disputes cause losses.

3. Who we serve

Farmers / villagers

Often low-literacy. Need zero-friction ordering: a Hindi voice note or a photo of the broken part. Primary channel: WhatsApp; later, a simple app.

Mistris (mechanics)

Power users who order repeatedly and hold farmer trust. Get a dedicated Android app with a catalog, and a wallet with 3–5% cashback on orders they place. They are partners, never bypassed.

Every order is tagged by where it came from (WhatsApp / app / counter) and who placed it (farmer / mistri), so we always know what is working.

4. How an order flows (the KisanGati loop)

Farmer sends WhatsApp message (text / voice / photo) │ ▼ Shop sees it instantly in the dashboard ──► (AI suggests the likely part) │ ▼ Staff confirm the part + price ──► send order summary + UPI QR in the chat │ ▼ Farmer pays by UPI (PhonePe / GPay / Paytm) ──► staff confirm payment received │ ▼ Shop packs + prints a label (bus route + conductor + 4-digit OTP) │ ▼ Part goes on the bus ──► farmer gives the OTP at the village stop to collect │ ▼ Delivered ✓ (mistri cashback credited, if a mistri placed it)

No part is packed before payment is confirmed. This removes the cash-on-delivery risk and matches how the shop's current UPI clients already work.

5. What makes KisanGati different

  • Order without knowing the part — send a voice note or a photo; the shop (and, over time, the AI) figures out what it is.
  • Delivery on the existing bus network — no new fleet, no courier cost; collected at the village stop with a secret code.
  • Upfront UPI only — no bad debt, no failed pickups.
  • Mistri-friendly — mechanics earn commission and stay central, instead of being cut out.
  • A data moat over time — every photo the shop resolves teaches an AI that will eventually identify parts automatically.

7. Product requirements (what we will build)

7.1 Farmer — WhatsApp (first) and app (later)

  • Send text, Hindi voice note, or photo of the part / engine nameplate.
  • Get an automatic acknowledgement in Hindi.
  • Receive an order summary with a Confirm button and a UPI QR in the chat.
  • Receive the 4-digit collection OTP when the part is dispatched, and a delivery confirmation.
  • App version (later): the same flow with big icons and Hindi voice, plus order history.

7.2 Mistri — Android app

  • Phone + OTP login.
  • Browse catalog by brand / model; cart and quick reorder.
  • UPI checkout; order tracking with notifications.
  • Wallet: 3–5% cashback on app-placed orders, with UPI payout.
  • Refer a farmer (mistri stays in the loop).

7.3 Shop — web dashboard (operated by us)

  • One inbox for all orders (WhatsApp + app + counter).
  • Play the voice note / view the photo; see the AI's suggested parts.
  • Match to a catalog item, set price, send the UPI QR to the chat.
  • Mark payment received; print the dispatch label; mark dispatched / delivered.
  • Onboard a walk-in customer to both the app and WhatsApp at the counter.
  • Daily sales and simple analytics.

7.4 AI part identification (the long-term moat)

  • Goal: from a photo (+ optional voice note), identify brand, part type, and key specs, and suggest the exact catalog item — even when the farmer can't name it.
  • Honest limits: brand and part type from a photo are achievable; fine specs like exact teeth count from a casual, greasy photo are hard and will stay human-checked at first.
  • Plan: start now using online catalog images + a modern AI vision model to assist staff, while every real photo the shop resolves is saved to train a better, KisanGati-owned model over time. (Online images help bootstrap but do not replace real farmer photos.)

7.5 Payments

  • Now: a UPI QR / link is sent in the chat; the customer pays with any UPI app; staff confirm receipt manually. No gateway, no fees — matches the shop's current practice.
  • Later: a payment gateway (e.g. Razorpay) can auto-confirm payments; the system is built so this is a simple switch, not a rebuild.

7.6 Delivery

  • Reuse the existing bus-conductor tie-ups. Delivery to the village bus stop.
  • Claimed with a 4-digit OTP handed to the conductor / given to the farmer.

9.2 What the market data tells us (and what it doesn't)

We researched comparable Indian rural / agri-commerce businesses. Two honest conclusions shape the plan:

  • Rural commerce is a volume game, not a high-ticket one. Tier-2/3 towns drive ~3 of every 5 online orders in India, at roughly the same basket size as cities (Bain–Flipkart). Growth comes from more orders, not bigger ones.
  • Margins are structurally thin sector-wide. B2B agri take-rates run ~3–5%; players survive on volume, repeat buyers, and private label — not on fat per-order profit. KisanGati's advantage is that it rides an existing shop's real part margin and a near-free bus last-mile, so its per-order economics can be healthier than pure-play startups.

Note: tractor spare-parts order values are not published anywhere — no competitor or report discloses them. So AOV and margin must come from the father's real prices (the pending input), not from the market. That is expected and fine.

Build order (money loop before polish)

  1. Server + database — the shared brain.
  2. WhatsApp intake — prove farmers will order this way.
  3. Shop web dashboard — process real orders end to end.
  4. AI assist (in parallel) — help staff match parts; collect training data from day one.
  5. Farmer + mistri apps — once the loop works with real money.

Deferred until the loop works: the custom-trained AI model, the mistri services marketplace, opening the platform to other shops, and any large-scale deployment.
